# Qualification-test answer key — coordinator only

This key applies to the fourth-page task-understanding test in both guides. Each question has four options and one correct answer. Keep this key separate from annotator copies.

## Readiness decision

Confirm readiness once all 10 answers are correct. A blank answer or more than one choice is incomplete. If a response reveals a misunderstanding, clarify the rule and ask the annotator to explain it in their own words before selecting an answer again. Corrected responses can satisfy the check; confirm understanding before annotation begins.

## Correct answers and explanations

| Question | Correct option and explanation |
---|---|
 1. For an item you can assess, what must you record? | **C — Eight 1–5 scores, reasons and needed corrections**. Record each of the eight criterion scores separately, with brief reasons and any needed corrections. |
 2. How should you distinguish scores 4 and 5? | **A — 4: fully successful; 5: an explained exceptional strength**. Fully successful ordinary work can receive 4. A 5 requires a specific strength beyond ordinary good work, with an explanation. |
 3. How should an important unsupported assertion affect scoring? | **D — Give Grounding at most 2; judge other criteria separately**. An important unsupported assertion warrants at most 2 for Grounding. Other criteria are assessed independently; the factual flaw need not affect every score. |
 4. No relevant numbers or references apply. What do you record for Numerical fidelity? | **B — 4 and “not applicable”**. The guide specifies score 4 with the notation “not applicable” when no relevant numerical or reference content applies. |
 5. When should you use “cannot assess”? | **D — Needed material or expertise is missing; explain why**. Use cannot assess only when necessary material or expertise is missing, with a reason. Known lack of support or poor writing is a scoring problem. |
 6. What do you use to judge factual support and Linguistic quality, respectively? | **A — Supplied text; the question’s original language**. Factual support must come from the supplied text. Assess language in the original question, rather than only its English translation. |
 7. When checking whether a question stands on its own, how should you handle missing context? | **C — Judge only the information in the question**. For the standalone-understanding check, judge the question without filling in details from the source. Continue to use the document for the other checks that require it. |
 8. Before lowering a score because an answer is too long, what should you check? | **B — Whether a shorter continuous passage preserves all needed information and qualifications**. Do not penalize length alone. Identify a shorter continuous passage that still contains the requested information and essential qualifications. |
 9. What should a useful explanation of a score include? | **C — A specific strength or problem relevant to that criterion**. Explain a specific relevant strength or problem, using the question, answer or document as appropriate. Note a correction when needed. |
 10. What should you do when an answer provides only part of the requested information? | **B — Identify what is missing and lower the affected scores appropriately**. Identify missing requested details and score affected criteria according to the importance of the omission. Do not automatically lower unrelated scores or treat a known omission as unassessable. |

For legal item 6, “supplied text” includes the historical context and permitted supplied material described in the guide. For chemistry, the source must support the factual claims; this does not prohibit using professional knowledge to interpret it.
